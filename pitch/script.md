# Before the Group Chat: Pitch Script (pointers)

These are talking points, not lines to memorise. Hit each point in your own words. Lines in quotes are optional anchors, worth saying close to word for word.

**Length:** 11 slides, about 6:30, with 30 seconds spare inside the 7-minute limit.
**Clicks:** `▸` marks one click. Every slide waits for your click, so you set the pace.
**Speakers:** suggested 3-way split, shown as **A / B / C** below. Adjust it to your team size.

**One name for one thing.** Always say **"the scorecard"**: the ten things every first meeting should settle. Each table's student leader ticks it during the round and reads out the score. Never call it a sheet, a checklist or a template.

## 0 · Title card · before the clock starts

- Have it on screen while the panel settles in. It doesn't count towards the 7 minutes.
- When you're ready to start, click to slide 1 and begin.

## 1 · The chat that died · 0:00–0:40 · A

- Open on the phone. Say nothing for a beat and let it sit.
- ▸ The messages type in. It's a normal first meeting: everyone's friendly and someone makes a group chat.
- ▸ "Seen by 4", then weeks 4 and 7 with nothing in between. Ask who's been in this chat.
- ▸ Week 8: "is anyone working on this??" Pause and let the room react.
- ▸ The headline lands.
  - Nothing went wrong in week 8. Week 1 just never set anything up.
  - Who's working Thursdays, who wants a 7, who goes quiet: nobody asked.
- Anchor: *"Teams don't fail in week 8. They fail in week 1."*

## 2 · First year is full of first meetings · 0:40–1:05 · A

- Audience: **first-year EAIT students, in O-Week**.
- ▸ The group chats stack up and the counter hits 12: ENGG1100, COMP1100, DECO1800, a society committee, a hackathon team. Every one of them starts with a first meeting, and most of them in week 1 or 2.
- Call back to slide 1: each of these chats started exactly like the one that died.
- About a dozen in year one is our estimate, so say "about a dozen".
- ▸ Why now: they have no habits yet, good or bad. Nobody teaches how to start a group.

## 3 · The scorecard · 1:05–1:50 · A

- The skill, scoped tight: **lead a group's first meeting, from strangers to a shared plan in 15 minutes**.
- "A good first meeting" isn't vague. It's ten things every first meeting should settle, and that's our scorecard.
- ▸ The five questions appear. Read the questions only, not every box:
  - Who are we?
  - What's the goal?
  - Who does what?
  - When do we work?
  - What if it goes wrong?
- Give one example box so it feels real: *"Our deadline, before the real one."* That's the box nobody settles, and why week 8 happens.
- ▸ Eight boxes tick. Two stay empty, because nobody gets a perfect score, and 8 of 10 is the Round 2 target.
- Hand-off: "So how does the hour run?"

## 4 · One hour, two first meetings · 1:50–2:35 · A

The full wording is in `scripts/section-4-the-hour.md`, which stays local only. The timeline builds one block per click, so say each beat as its block appears. The duration shows above the block, and the detail drops in below.

- Open on the empty hour: one 60-minute workshop that simulates a real first meeting, twice.
- ▸ **Hook:** hands up if you've been ghosted in the team group chat, or done everyone's work the night before.
- ▸ **Round 1:** groups of five, a fictional coursework brief, their first ever meeting, scored live by the student leader.
- ▸ **Score:** they hear their score out of 10.
- ▸ **Teach:** a short teach built around the scorecard.
- ▸ **Round 2:** a student society, so the skills have to transfer, not just be copied.
- ▸ **Pledge:** a reflective pledge card for their next group project.
- ▸ 10 minutes of listening, 50 of doing.

## 5 · Five tables, seven student leaders · 2:35–2:50 · A → hand to B

**Switch to this slide** as you start describing the tables, with "Here's the room."
- ▸ The leaders appear: five participants and a student leader at every table, plus two more on the floor.
- ▸ The label flips to Round 2: same room for both rounds.
- Hand over to B.

## 6 · Five players, five secrets · 2:50–3:15 · B

- Each player gets a secret role card.
- ▸ The cards flip. Name two or three as they land:
  - the shift worker who's busy Thursday nights
  - the one flying home in week 5
  - the one who says "all good" but isn't
- Every card hides something that belongs in a box on the scorecard. The shift worker's Thursdays go under "everyone's busy weeks", and the HD Chaser is "what's the goal?". Point out this link; it shows the activity was designed around the scorecard.
- They're fictional on purpose, so nobody shares their own life.
- ▸ Nobody mentions theirs unless someone asks, and in Round 1 almost nobody does. That's why Round 1 scores come back low.

## 7 · Three moves, any seat · 3:15–4:15 · B → hand to C

- **Live moment.** Before clicking, hand a judge the printed Quiet One card.
- ▸ The card flips on screen. Turn to the judge.
- ▸ Model the move to the judge, then let them answer in character:
  - *"We haven't heard from you yet. What do you think?"*
- Then name the three moves and the inner skill each one trains:
  - ▸ **Open the meeting** trains self mastery. "Can we take 10 minutes to plan before the group chat?" That one sentence is the name of the workshop.
  - ▸ **Draw in a quiet voice** trains empathy. Point at what just happened with the judge.
  - ▸ **Name the tension** trains emotional awareness. It's the HD Chaser's card: "some of us want a 7, some want a pass", said out loud in week 1 rather than discovered in week 8.
- ▸ **Resilience:** "fail safely in Round 1, fix it in Round 2." Round 1 is meant to go badly, and the room is the safe place for that to happen.
- Backup if the judge won't play: a teammate plays the Quiet One.
- Hand-off: "So how do we know it worked?"

## 8 · Same people, thirty minutes apart · 4:15–5:05 · C

- ▸ Round 1 scores are low, because nobody asks the five questions.
- ▸ Round 2 clears the target of 8. ▸ Table 1 goes from 3 to 9.
- **Be upfront:** these numbers are illustrative until our pilot. If we've piloted, swap in the real ones and lead with them.
- ▸ These four measures are our SMART objectives. Say each one with **what's counted, who counts it, and when**:
  - **Scorecard:** 8 of 10 for every group in Round 2, by the end of the hour, ticked live by the table's student leader.
  - **Leader-move tally:** every player makes 2 of the 3 moves in Round 2, also tallied by the student leader.
  - **Week 4 form:** half of the people who reply led or shaped a real first meeting within 4 weeks.
  - **Exit card:** confidence from 1 to 5, before versus now. This one is a bonus measure.
- Name the obvious objection before they do: some of the gain is just doing it twice. That's why Round 2 is a different kind of group.

## 9 · How it gets into first year · 5:05–5:30 · C

The full wording is in `scripts/section-9-into-first-year.md`.
- ▸ **UQ Employability:** our first step is to ask them to help fund and set up the event.
- ▸ **ENGG1100 and COMP1100:** we reach out to the course coordinators and mentors, so students hear about it from inside their first team-project courses.
- ▸ **O-Week to week 2:** it runs alongside those courses, starting in orientation week before uni begins.
- ▸ The strip fills in: three sessions, before and alongside the first group projects.

## 10 · $95 and a tutorial room · 5:30–6:00 · C

- ▸ The receipt: printing (scorecards, role cards and pledge cards), snacks, and a small prize to get people to fill in the week 4 form. **$95 a session.**
- ▸ Seven volunteer student leaders, five at tables and two on the floor, with a 30-minute briefing.
- ▸ Any tutorial room with five tables and a screen.
- ▸ O-Week to week 2, three sessions, alongside ENGG1100 and COMP1100.
- The point: this could run next O-Week with nothing new built.

## 11 · Close · 6:00–6:30 · C

- Everyone leaves with a blank scorecard for their real team's first meeting, plus this pledge.
- ▸ ▸ ▸ Read the pledge lines as they write themselves. Slow down here.
- ▸ The name appears: **Before the Group Chat**. Stop talking for a beat.
- Close the loop with slide 1: that chat died because nobody led the meeting before it.
- Anchor: *"You don't need a title to lead. Be the one who starts it."* Then "Thank you."

## If you're running long

Cut in this order:
1. Slide 5: click both builds while saying the one sentence.
2. Slide 4: click through the blocks briskly and only speak to Round 1, Teach and Round 2.
3. Slide 10: show the receipt total only and skip the icons.

**Never cut** the scorecard (slide 3), the live moment (slide 7) or the close.
