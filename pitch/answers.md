# Q&A screen: prepared answers

These five questions are on the last slide. None of the answers appear on the slides, so a judge asking one is genuinely curious, and we're ready. Aim for about 20 seconds each. Answer, then stop; don't re-pitch.

---

## 1. What about the person who wants a 7 and won't budge? · B

They don't have to budge, and we never ask them to. What the workshop changes is *when* the clash happens: week 1 instead of week 8.

- **"Name the tension"** puts it on the table in the first meeting.
- **"What's the goal?"** gets a target the whole group says out loud, so nobody is guessing.
- **"Who does what?"** lets the 7-chaser own the parts their grade depends on, while everyone else commits to a clear minimum.

They end up fine not because everyone agrees, but because they know where everyone stands from day one.

## 2. Why would a first-year give up an hour of O-Week for this? · A

Because it isn't a teamwork lecture. It's a rehearsal of a meeting they're about to have for real.

- Their own course coordinators and mentors tell them about it, inside ENGG1100 and COMP1100, right before their first group projects.
- It's 50 minutes of doing, with role cards and a score. It feels like a game, not a seminar.
- They walk out with a scorecard to use in their real first meeting that same week.

And there are snacks.

## 3. What if a student leader is a weak facilitator? · C

The design doesn't rely on great facilitators.

- The **scorecard** carries the structure. The leader ticks fixed boxes and reads out a score; they don't need to teach at the table.
- All seven leaders get a **30-minute briefing**, where they practise scoring one round with a sample meeting.
- The **two floor leaders** are the backstop. The roaming one spot-checks tables and steps in wherever a table stalls.

## 4. Doesn't ENGG1100 already teach teamwork? · A

It teaches teamwork; we rehearse the first meeting. They complement each other.

- Courses explain how teams *should* work. Nobody practises the first meeting itself before it counts.
- We'd line the scorecard up with whatever team process the course already uses, so students hear one consistent message, not a competing one.

## 5. What if UQ Employability says no? · C

Then it still runs. It costs $95 a session, and it's run by student volunteers.

- **Co-host with a student society.** Societies already run O-Week events, and the student leaders can come from their committees.
- **Run it inside a course.** If a coordinator gives us a week 1 workshop slot, the room and the audience come with it.
- **Ask the faculty.** EAIT runs first-year transition events every year; at this price, this is an easy one to add.

UQ Employability is the best fit, not the only one.
