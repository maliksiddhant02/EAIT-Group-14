# Before the Group Chat: Pitch Storyboard

> *Every team you'll ever be on is decided in its first meeting. Most of us never learn to lead one.*

**Format:** a PowerPoint pitch to the judging panel, 5 to 7 minutes. **Target: 6:30.**
**Slides:** 11 in the deck. Since this storyboard was written: a First-Meeting Sheet slide and a "three numbers" slide replaced the old skill slide, slide 4 was split in two, and a "five secrets" slide was added. The talking points are in [`pitch-script.md`](../pitch/script.md), which replaces the **Say** blocks below.
**Deck:** [`pitch-deck.html`](../pitch/deck.html) is now the source of truth. Since this storyboard was written, the deck dropped the section labels, footer and small text, switched to Poppins, uses 4 big doors on slide 2, and shows 4 measures instead of the paper props on slide 6.
**Story in one line:** a group chat dies in week 8 → it was decided in week 1 → we give first-years 12 minutes of practice that change it → here's the proof and the price.

The deck build spec is in [`claude-design-prompt.md`](claude-design-prompt-v1.md). This file is the story: what the audience sees, what we say, and why each slide exists.

## Slide 1: The chat that died · 0:00–0:40

**Purpose:** every judge has a group-project horror story. Trigger it in 10 seconds.

**On screen:** a single phone outline, centred on a blank beige background, showing a group chat.

**Animation (on clicks):**
1. Messages type in one at a time, with a typing indicator before each:
   *"hey team 👋" · "hi!" · "let's make a group chat" · "👍"*
2. A small label above the phone flips **Week 1 → Week 4 → Week 7**, and the chat goes quiet. Under the last message, *"Seen by 4"* fades in.
3. Week 8: one new message drops in, alone: *"is anyone working on this?? due friday"*
4. The phone slides left. On the right, the headline fades in: **Teams don't fail in week 8. They fail in week 1.**

**Say:**
> "Every one of us has been in this chat. The first meeting ended with 'let's make a group chat', and nobody asked who's working Thursdays, who wants a 7, or who'll go quiet. By week 8, it's too late. We think the fix is the first 12 minutes."

**Hand-off line:** *"So who needs this most?"*

## Slide 2: 12 first meetings · 0:40–1:15

**Purpose:** a specific audience and why *now*.

**On screen:** a simple line-drawn student figure on the left. A horizontal line runs out to the right: their first year.

**Animation:**
1. The figure draws in, labelled **First-year EAIT · O-Week**.
2. Along the timeline, small door icons pop in one by one, each with a label: *ENGG1100 team · COMP1100 team · DECO1800 team · Engineering society committee · Study group · Hackathon team…* until there are **12 doors**.
3. A counter in the corner ticks **0 → 12**, with the caption *first meetings in year one*.
4. A thin underline sweeps under the figure: **No habits yet, good or bad. Nobody teaches this.**

**Say:**
> "Our audience is first-year EAIT students in O-Week. In their first year alone, they'll sit through about a dozen first meetings: course teams, club committees, hackathons. They have no habits yet, so this is the cheapest moment to teach it."

**Note:** "12" is our estimate. Say "about a dozen", or count a real first-year timetable before the pitch.

## Slide 3: Strangers → agreement in 12 minutes · 1:15–2:05

**Purpose:** one well-scoped skill and three objectives with numbers in them.

**On screen:** the top half is the skill. The bottom half has 3 objective cards.

**Animation:**
1. Five scattered dots (strangers) drift together into a circle, and a small document icon appears in the middle. Caption: **Lead a group's first meeting: strangers to a written agreement in 12 minutes.**
2. A subtitle fades in: *You don't need a title to lead. You need to start the meeting properly.*
3. The objective cards rise in one at a time, each with a number that counts up:
   - **8/10**: every group's Round 2 agreement hits 8 of the 10 checklist items, up from Round 1. *(Group · by end of hour)*
   - **2 of 3**: every player makes 2 of the 3 leader moves in Round 2. *(Individual · by end of hour)*
   - **50%**: half of follow-up respondents led or shaped a real first meeting with the template. *(Lasting · within 4 weeks)*

**Say:**
> "The skill is narrow on purpose: lead one first meeting well. We measure it three ways: the group, the individual, and four weeks later."

## Slide 4: The hour · 2:05–3:20

**Purpose:** show how it runs, minute by minute, and where people stand in the room.

**On screen:** two figures. The top one is a 60-minute timeline bar. The bottom one is a top-down map of the room.

**Animation:**
1. **Timeline:** a bar draws left to right in segments, each labelled as it appears:
   `Hook 5 · Round 1: cold 15 · Reveal 10 · Teach 8 · Round 2: new group 14 · Score + pledge 8`
   The *doing* segments fill solid and the talking segments stay outlined. A bracket appears: **52 of 60 minutes, participants are doing, not listening.**
2. **Loop diagram** (small, to the right of the bar): *Cold → Reveal → Teach → Again* drawn as a circular arrow, then *Pledge* exiting the loop.
3. **Room map:** five round tables fade in, each with 6 seat-dots. At each table, 5 dots are filled (players) and 1 is a hollow ring (observer). **A** sits at the front by the screen. **B**'s icon moves around the tables along a dotted path, on loop.
4. Round 2 callout: Table 1's label switches from *"Course team"* to *"Club committee"*. Caption: **Round 2 is a different kind of group, to show the skill transfers.**

**Say:**
> "Thirty students, five tables. Round 1, they run a first meeting cold, and nobody's assigned to lead. Then the reveal: secret role cards are read out, and they score their own plan. Eight minutes of teaching. Then Round 2, with a different kind of group: a club committee instead of a course team. Two Ambassadors run it: one at the front, one moving between tables."

**Why it's built this way (only if asked, or in Q&A):** no leader is assigned so everyone has to step up. The context changes in Round 2 so we're testing transfer, not repetition.

## Slide 5: Play a card · 3:20–4:35

**Purpose:** the judges *feel* the activity. It's the strongest slide in the deck.

**Live moment (30 sec):** before the slide, one presenter hands a printed role card to a judge.

**On screen:**
1. A role card, face down (patterned back), centred.
2. **Click:** the card **flips** to show: **THE QUIET ONE** · *You have good ideas but only speak when someone asks you directly.*
3. Presenter turns to the judge and models the move: *"We haven't heard from you yet. What works for you?"* The judge responds in character.
4. **Click:** the card shrinks to the left. On the right, three speech bubbles appear one at a time, each tagged with its inner skill:

| Bubble | Tag |
|---|---|
| "Can we spend 10 minutes agreeing how this'll work?" | **Open the meeting** · *Self mastery* |
| "We haven't heard from you yet. What works for you?" | **Draw in a quiet voice** · *Empathy* |
| "Sounds like some want a 7 and some want a pass. Let's sort it now." | **Name the tension** · *Emotional awareness* |

5. A final line under the bubbles: *Resilience: you do it badly in Round 1, and do it again.*

**Say:**
> "That's the whole activity. Five people, five secret cards: the shift worker, the HD chaser, the one flying home in week 5. Nobody mentions theirs unless someone asks. Round 1 almost nobody asks. In the reveal, they find out what they missed. Then we teach three moves, each one an inner skill, and they try again."

**Backup if no judge plays along:** the presenter reads the card aloud and a teammate plays the Quiet One.

## Slide 6: 3 → 9 · 4:35–5:20

**Purpose:** a before/after measure, shown as the actual tools.

**On screen:** the left half is a bar chart. The right half shows the two paper tools.

**Animation:**
1. Five pairs of bars, one pair per table. The Round 1 bars (outlined) grow first and stop low. The Round 2 bars (solid accent) grow past a dashed line labelled **8/10 target**.
2. One pair is highlighted: **3 → 9**.
3. The scoring checklist slides in on the right as a paper sheet with 10 tick boxes, which tick themselves one by one.
4. The exit card slides in under it: *Confidence to lead a first meeting: before ①②③④⑤ / now ①②③④⑤*
5. A small timeline at the bottom: **Today → Week 4 follow-up form**.

**Say:**
> "Everything is measured in the room. The checklist score goes from Round 1 to Round 2, the observer tallies the leader moves, and the exit card captures confidence. Four weeks later, a three-question form asks: did you lead a first meeting?"

**Honesty note:** the 3 → 9 bars are **illustrative until we pilot**. If we pilot (strongly recommended), swap in the real numbers and say "We ran it with six people." Otherwise, label the chart *Illustrative*.

**Planning and Reflection line (one sentence):** *"Some of the gain is just doing it twice, which is why Round 2 uses a different kind of group."*

## Slide 8: Be the one who starts it · 6:00–6:30

**Purpose:** end on what a participant walks out with.

**On screen:** a single pledge card, centred, at business-card scale.

**Animation:**
1. The three lines **handwrite themselves** onto the card:
   *In my next first meeting, I'll open by* ___ · *I'll make sure* ___ *gets heard* · *When I sense tension, I'll say* ___
2. The card shrinks. The title fades in large: **Before the Group Chat**, with *Be the one who starts it.* underneath.

**Say:**
> "Every participant leaves with this card and a blank template, and a first meeting that same week to use it in. You don't need a title to lead. You just need to be the one who starts it. Thank you."

- [ ] Rehearse to **6:30** with the clicker. Every animation is on-click, so if we're running over, skip a build.
