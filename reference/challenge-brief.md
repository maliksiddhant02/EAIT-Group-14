# Inside Out Impact Challenge: Brief

Nov 23, 2026 · @phoneix

Your team pitches a 60-minute workshop (or a short series) that teaches fellow EAIT students a skill, in a 5 to 7 minute PowerPoint to a judging panel.

## The challenge

Judges reward a thoughtful, effective and practical learning experience, not an impressive skill. The slides say this outright: prioritise the design over the complexity of the skill.

- **Theme:** "Leading out Loud". Technical skill is useless if you can't communicate it, so the challenge links inner leadership skills to how they show up in teamwork, learning and design.
- **Inner skills named:** resilience, empathy, emotional awareness, self mastery.
- **What's being tested:** can you spot a real learning need, understand an audience, and design an engaging, practical workshop that builds a useful skill.
- **Who's listening:** the panel takes the view of your teammates or potential collaborators. Explain how it works; don't sell it.

## What you build

One 60-minute workshop, or a series, teaching other students a skill you're passionate about. You don't need every detail finalised, but the panel must understand the experience and what people walk away with.

1. **Pick a skill you can confidently teach.** The slide's examples: CAD, coding, public speaking, problem solving, networking.
2. **Set a learning outcome and workshop goals.** What will participants learn, and how will you know they've learned it?
3. **Design an interactive learning experience.** A game, simulation, practical task or structured group discussion, and how it gets people to the outcome.

## What the pitch must cover

The deliverable is a 5 to 7 minute PowerPoint. Slides 4 and 5 list overlapping requirements; together they come to seven topics.

| Topic | What the pitch has to answer | Slide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Target audience | Who is it for, why them, and why would they benefit? | 4, 5 |
| Skill focus | What skill, and why is it relevant to that audience? | 4, 5 |
| SMART learning objectives | What can participants do by the end, stated so it's measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound? | 4, 5 |
| Workshop structure | How does the hour run? If it's a series, what happens in each session? | 4, 5 |
| Interactive activity | What do participants actually do, and how does it build the outcome? | 4, 5 |
| Measuring success | How will you know they achieved the outcome? | 4 |
| Feasibility | What does it cost, would people actually want it, and can it run at UQ? | 5 |

## How it's judged

Five criteria, with no weights given, so assume they count equally. Four of the five are about the design itself; only one is about delivery.

| Criterion | What judges look for | What it means for your pitch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clarity of Purpose | A well-scoped, measurable skill relevant to EAIT students; real understanding of the audience's needs | Name one specific audience and one specific pain they have. "All students" and "communication" are too broad. |
| Workshop Design and Engagement | A structured, engaging, interactive experience that supports the outcome | Participants should be doing something for most of the hour, and every activity should map to the objective. |
| Learning Objectives | Clear, measurable, achievable SMART objectives that match the audience's needs | Write objectives with a number and a deadline in them, e.g. "by the end of the hour, each participant will..." |
| Planning and Reflection | Critical thinking, justified design decisions, and a way to measure learning | Explain why you chose this over alternatives, show costs, and measure before and after. |
| Communication and Presentation | A clear, engaging, cohesive, audience-aware presentation | Keep to time, tell one story, and show the activity rather than describing it. |

## A 7-minute run sheet

One slide per required topic, in the order a collaborator would ask about them. Aim for 6:30 so you have buffer.

1. **Hook (0:00 to 0:45):** one real moment where your audience struggles. A quote, a stat or a 10-second story.
2. **Audience (0:45 to 1:30):** who exactly, when in their degree, and why they need this now.
3. **Skill and SMART objectives (1:30 to 2:30):** the skill in one line, then 2 or 3 objectives with numbers in them.
4. **Workshop structure (2:30 to 3:45):** the hour as a simple timeline, minute by minute.
5. **Interactive activity (3:45 to 5:00):** what participants do. Best case, give the judges a 30-second taste of it.
6. **Measuring success (5:00 to 5:45):** show the actual rubric or exit card, plus a before-and-after comparison.
7. **Feasibility (5:45 to 6:30):** cost table, who runs it, where and when at UQ.
8. **Close (6:30 to 7:00):** what a participant walks out able to do.

## Unclear in the brief

The biggest gap is whether the skill has to tie back to the four inner skills. Worth asking the organisers before you lock an idea.

- [ ] **Theme vs task:** slides 1 and 2 frame the challenge around resilience, empathy, emotional awareness and self mastery, but the task allows any skill (CAD, coding). Safe play: a practical skill taught through one of those inner skills.
- [ ] **Weights:** no weighting is given for the five criteria. Are they equal?
- [ ] **Slide 5, item 1:** "Select the Workshop Focus" asks who the workshop is for, which repeats item 2. Probably meant to be the skill focus.
- [ ] **SMART:** slide 5 spells the S as "Structured". The usual meaning is Specific; write objectives that are both.
- [ ] **Format:** is there Q&A after the 5 to 7 minutes, is the deck submitted beforehand, and when is the pitch?
