# Before the Group Chat: Project Brief (v2, leadership framing)

Working brief for Group 14. Sources: `../reference/challenge-slides.pdf` (the official task), `../reference/challenge-brief.md`, `../reference/idea-overview-v1.md` and `../reference/idea-shortlist.md`.

**What we're delivering:** a 5 to 7 minute PowerPoint pitch to a judging panel (see §8). We don't need a video, because the challenge asks for slides.

**What changed from v1:** the workshop now teaches you to **lead** a first meeting, not just to be a good teammate in one. §2 shows how this change scores against the rubric.

---

## 1. The idea in one breath

> **Teams fail in week 1, not week 8.** That's when the first meeting ends with "let's make a group chat" and nothing else. Before the Group Chat is a 60-minute workshop where first-year EAIT students practise **leading** a group's first meeting twice. The first time they go in cold. The second time they have a simple toolkit, and they watch their own score go up.

**Pitch line:** *"You don't need a title to lead. You need to be the one who starts the first meeting properly."*

**Theme fit ("Leading out Loud"):** the first meeting is the earliest leadership moment a student will ever get, and it's literally leading out loud: being the person who says *"Can we spend 10 minutes agreeing how this will work?"*

**"Whoever it's with":** the skill transfers to any new group, such as a course team, a club committee, a hackathon team or later an internship project. We **show** this inside the hour: Round 1 is a course project and Round 2 is a club committee. We don't make it the audience, because a broad audience costs marks (see §2).

The flow: **Cold run → Reveal → Teach → Run again in a new context → Pledge**

---

## 2. How the change stands against the rubric

The rubric has 5 criteria with no weights given, so I've treated them as equal. Arrows compare v2 with the original "better teammate" version, which `../reference/idea-shortlist.md` scored at 96/100.

| Criterion | v1: better teammate | v2: lead the first meeting | Change | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Clarity of Purpose** | Specific audience, specific pain (ENGG1100 teams) | Same audience. The skill is "lead a first meeting", with the course team as the main example | **= / ▼ risk** | The rubric says *"All students" and "communication" are too broad.* "Leadership, with anyone" would be too broad as well. Keeping the audience at first-year EAIT and the skill at one moment (the first meeting) holds the score. |
| **Workshop Design and Engagement** | Players practise teammate moves | No leader is assigned, so **everyone** has to step up. Round 2 switches context | **▲** | Every player stays active because nobody can sit back and wait for a chair. The context switch keeps Round 2 fresh. |
| **Learning Objectives** | "Team used a charter" (depends on others) | "*You* led or shaped a first meeting" (depends on you) | **▲** | The 4-week objective now measures what the participant did, which is more achievable and more honest. |
| **Planning and Reflection** | Before/after measured inside the hour | Before/after **plus a transfer test** (a different context in Round 2) | **▲** | Improving in a *new* context is stronger evidence than repeating the same task, so it answers the "it's just practice" objection. |
| **Communication and Presentation** | Good story | Same story, and it matches the challenge title | **▲** | "Leading out Loud" plus a workshop about leading a first meeting is one clear story. |

**Verdict:** v2 is a net gain on 4 of the 5 criteria. The **only way it loses marks is if we widen the audience** to "all students, any meeting". The audience stays first-year EAIT. "Whoever it's with" is shown in Round 2, not claimed in the audience.

## 3. At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Audience** | First-year EAIT students in O-Week or weeks 1 to 2, just before their first team project (ENGG1100, COMP1100, DECO1800) and first club involvement |
| **Why them, why now** | They're about to sit through a dozen first meetings, with teams, clubs and study groups, and they have no habits yet, good or bad. Nobody teaches this. |
| **Skill** | Leading a group's first meeting: getting a new group from strangers to a written agreement in 12 minutes |
| **Inner skills** | **Empathy** (drawing in quiet teammates), **emotional awareness** (naming tension early), **self mastery** (stepping up and owning commitments). **Resilience** comes from doing it badly, reflecting, and going again. |
| **Format** | One 60-minute session, 25 participants at 5 tables of 5, with a student leader at each table |
| **Run by** | 1 EAIT Ambassador at the front, plus 5 student leaders: volunteers from later years, one per table |
| **Where / when** | A booked tutorial room in week 1, run 3 times across the week, promoted through ENGG1100 / COMP1100 / DECO1800 course announcements and Ambassador channels |
| **Cost** | About $95 per session (§7) |
| **Takeaway** | A blank First-Meeting Sheet and a 3-line leader pledge |

---

## 4. SMART objectives

1. **Group:** by the end of the 60 minutes, each group's Round 2 agreement ticks **at least 8 of the 10** boxes on the First-Meeting Sheet, and scores higher than its Round 1 agreement.
2. **Individual:** in Round 2, **every player makes at least 2 of the 3 leader moves** (open the meeting, draw in a quiet voice, name the tension), as tallied by their table's student leader. Most should be up from Round 1.
3. **Lasting:** every participant leaves with a written 3-line leader pledge. Within 4 weeks, **at least half of follow-up respondents** report they led or shaped a first meeting, in any group, using the sheet or their pledge.

---

## 5. How the hour runs

**The Ambassador** runs the session from the front: timer, slides and transitions.
**Five student leaders**, one per table, run their table: they hand out cards, keep the clock, tally leader moves, and lead the table's reveal. They're volunteers from later years, briefed for 30 minutes beforehand. Because they never play, all 25 participants get both rounds.

**Nobody is assigned as leader in either round.** That's deliberate, because the lesson is that anyone can lead from any seat.

| Time | Segment | What happens | Facilitator line (A) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | **Hook** | Mentimeter word cloud: *"Worst first meeting you've been in, in one word."* Read 3–4 aloud. | *"Every one of these needed one person to speak up first. Today that's you."* |
| 0:05–0:08 | **Round 1 setup** | Student leaders hand out Brief A (course project) and role cards set 1, and keep the tally sheet. | *"Read your card. Keep it secret. Play it honestly, not dramatically."* |
| 0:08–0:20 | **Round 1: cold (12 min)** | Groups run their first meeting with no guidance and write down whatever they agree. Student leaders tally leader moves. | *"You're a team as of now. Nobody's in charge. Go."* (2-min warning at 0:18) |
| 0:20–0:30 | **Reveal** | Players read their role cards aloud. The student leader answers *"Who led, if anyone?"* Tables **swap agreements with the next table** and score them, 1 point per box. | *"What did you learn about a teammate just now that never came up in the meeting? Whose job was it to ask?"* |
| 0:30–0:38 | **Teach (8 min)** | 2 slides: the 5 questions every first meeting answers and the 3 leader moves. Hand out the First-Meeting Sheet. | *"Leading a first meeting isn't about being the boss. It's about asking five questions and making sure everyone answers."* |
| 0:38–0:40 | **Round 2 setup** | Brief B (**club committee**, a different kind of group) and role cards set 2. Same student leader. | *"New group, new secrets, same clock. This time, someone at your table starts it properly."* |
| 0:40–0:52 | **Round 2: with tools (12 min)** | Same task, now with the sheet. Student leaders tally. | (2-min warning at 0:50) |
| 0:52–0:55 | **Score** | Swap and score again. A reads out each table's Round 1 → Round 2 score. | *"Table 3 went from 3 to 9. Different group, same people, 30 minutes apart."* |
| 0:55–1:00 | **Pledge + exit card** | Write the 3-line leader pledge, photograph it, and hand in the exit card. | *"You've got a first meeting this week. Be the one who starts it."* |

Teach is the only 8 minutes of listening (the Hook is a live poll), so participants are **doing something for about 52 of the 60 minutes**. Inner skills appear at these points:
- **Empathy:** Round 2, when players draw in the quiet voice.
- **Emotional awareness:** the Reveal at 0:20.
- **Self mastery:** opening the meeting in Round 2.
- **Resilience:** going again after a cold Round 1.

## 6. Materials

### 6.1 Core content: the First-Meeting Sheet (5 questions, 10 boxes) and 3 leader moves

**The First-Meeting Sheet** is one A4 page. Groups fill it in during each round, and the same page is what the next table scores, 1 point per box. Use this one name everywhere, never "template", "checklist" or "charter". The questions work for **any** group. On the slide, the boxes use the short wording; the descriptions below are the scoring rule.

| # | Question every first meeting answers | Boxes (1 point each) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Who are we?** | ① Contact channel + expected reply time agreed · ② Each person named one strength they bring |
| 2 | **What's the goal?** | ③ Target outcome agreed out loud (grade, event turnout, prize) · ④ One-sentence summary of the goal everyone agrees with |
| 3 | **Who does what?** | ⑤ At least one named role (e.g. coordinator, submitter, treasurer) · ⑥ First task assigned to a named person with a date |
| 4 | **When do we work?** | ⑦ Regular meeting time set · ⑧ An internal deadline before the real one |
| 5 | **What if it goes wrong?** | ⑨ Everyone's constraints / unavailable weeks written down · ⑩ A rule for missed deadlines or conflict (incl. who to escalate to) |

**3 leader moves.** The student leader tallies these for every player, because anyone can make them from any seat.

| Move | What it sounds like | Inner skill |
|---|---|---|
| **Open the meeting** | "Can we spend 10 minutes agreeing how this'll work? I've got five things." | Self mastery: stepping up |
| **Draw in a quiet voice** | "Sam, you haven't said much. What works for you?" | Empathy |
| **Name the tension** | "Sounds like some of us want a 7 and some want a pass. Let's sort that now, not in week 8." | Emotional awareness |

### 6.2 Mock briefs (half a page each)

- **Brief A (Round 1): course team.** "Campus Wayfinder." Design an app concept that helps new students find their classes. Deliverables: a 2,000-word report and a 5-minute pitch. Due week 6, worth 30%. Team of 5.
- **Brief B (Round 2): club committee.** "First-Year BBQ." You're 5 new committee members of an engineering society. Run a BBQ for 150 people in 3 weeks with a $500 budget. There's no grade, so everyone's a volunteer.

### 6.3 Secret role cards (2 sets × 5, fictional on purpose)

**Set 1 (course team)**
1. **The Shift Worker.** You work Tuesday and Thursday evenings and most Saturdays. You won't mention it unless someone asks about availability.
2. **The Quiet One.** You have good ideas but only speak when someone asks you directly.
3. **The HD Chaser.** You want a 7 and get quietly frustrated if others seem happy with a pass.
4. **The Away Teammate.** You fly home for a family wedding in week 5, the week before the deadline.
5. **The Overloaded.** You're doing 4 courses and this is your hardest one. You're anxious but you say "yeah, all good."

**Set 2 (club committee)**
1. **The Commuter.** You live 90 minutes away. Evening meetings on campus are impossible, but online works.
2. **The Takeover.** You'd rather do everything yourself than delegate, and you'll quietly resent it.
3. **The New-to-Australia.** You're unsure about local slang and hesitant to interrupt.
4. **Here for the Friends.** You joined to meet people. You'll help, but you're not taking on a big job.
5. **The Yes-Sayer.** You agree to everything and then quietly don't follow through.

## 7. Measuring success, feasibility and Q&A

### Measures

| Level | Measure | When | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Group | Sheet score, R1 vs R2 (peer-scored by the next table) | In room | Every group ≥ 8/10 in R2 and higher than its R1 score |
| Individual | Student leader's tally of leader moves, R1 vs R2 | In room | Every player ≥ 2 of 3 in R2 |
| Confidence | Exit card retrospective rating (before vs now, 1–5) | In room | Average rises by ≥ 1 point |
| Lasting | 3-question Microsoft Forms survey in week 4: *Did you lead or shape a first meeting? Did you use the sheet or your pledge? What happened?* | Week 4 | ≥ 50% of respondents say yes |

**Limitation we raise ourselves:** some of the Round 2 gain comes from simply doing it a second time. We reduce this by switching the context in Round 2, and by tracking boxes people only reach after the Teach segment (internal deadline, conflict rule).

### Likely panel questions

- **"Isn't 'leadership' too broad?"** We teach one leadership moment, the first meeting, with one sheet and three moves.
- **"Why not assign a leader each round?"** Then 4 of the 5 players sit back. Leaving it open makes everyone practise stepping up.
- **"Why not just hand out a template?"** Students ignore templates. Round 1 makes the gap felt first.
- **"Isn't Round 2 better just from practice?"** Partly, which is why Round 2 uses a different kind of group (see the limitation above).
- **"What if students won't open up?"** The role cards are fictional, so they talk about a character, not themselves.
- **"What if only 12 show up?"** It works with 2 to 5 tables of 4 to 6 people, with one student leader per table.
- **"Why Ambassadors and student leaders?"** Near-peer credibility. They've sat through these meetings themselves.
- **"What do the student leaders get out of it?"** Real practice leading a room: running a table, keeping time and giving feedback. The workshop builds leaders on both sides of the table, and the role works as a stepping stone to becoming an Ambassador.

### What we rejected, and why (for Planning and Reflection)

- **Lecture + template only:** there's no practice and nothing to measure.
- **2nd and 3rd years:** they already have habits. First-years have every first meeting still ahead of them.
- **"All students, any meeting":** this is too broad for Clarity of Purpose. We show transfer in Round 2 instead.
- **Say It Plainly and Review Without the Ego** (the other shortlisted ideas): both have softer measures, and Review needs heavy prep. Before the Group Chat has a before/after built into the hour.
